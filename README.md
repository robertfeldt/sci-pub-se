# "Scientific Publishing in SE" PhD Course

This is the home page for the PhD course "Scientific Publishing in SE" given by Robert Feldt.

**Quick links:** [Slides from all course instances](slides/slides_overview.md) · [Papers and readings, by course theme](papers/README.md)

## Introduction
The course is focused on seminars and discussion to help participants form their own views and answers, both on a theoretical and a practical level, to key questions like:
- What is a good scientific study in software engineering (SE)?
- What is a good SE paper?
- In what sense should my evidence support my claims?
- What is the difference between publishing in a journal and at a conference?
- Do different publication venues have different requirements and status? Where should I target my papers?
- How do review processes work, and how do I review papers?
- How is research and how are researchers assessed, and how is that changing?
- What is this thing called science? Is software engineering a science?
- How do AI tools change how we do, write up and review research, and what should we (not) use them for?

The course consists of discussion-based seminars to which participants do some light preparation. The idea is to give hands-on practical advice and show examples of how the software engineering research community functions. Participants bring one of their own papers (finished or in writing), which is used throughout the course, including for analysis with an AI-based claim–evidence tool. Since the course is an initial course, limited in size and scope, we go broad rather than in depth. But, at least, participants get a grounding in key activities and practices and can then go deeper as they see fit.

## Course goals
After completion of the course the participants are expected to be able to:
- Read SE research papers and describe their motivation, the methods used and the results achieved
- State the strong and weak points of an SE study, the threats to validity as well as how the study can be improved
- Identify the research paradigm and method used in an SE study and compare it to alternative methods
- Write good and constructive reviews of scientific papers in software engineering
- Apply review templates objectively even on their own research studies and papers
- Check whether the claims of a paper, including their own, are supported by its evidence
- Select relevant publication targets for their research
- Describe the strongest publication targets for SE in general as well as for their sub-area
- Write and update publication strategies for their research
- Define bibliometrics used to rank publication targets, journals and researchers, and discuss their limitations as well as current efforts to reform research assessment
- Discuss the opportunities and risks of using AI tools in research, writing and reviewing, and state their own policy for such use

## About Robert
Robert will introduce topics and lead discussions. He has a long background in Software Engineering both in academia and with 35+ years of experience from software & AI consulting. He has high standards and can be demanding but he really wants you to develop and grow and will go to great lengths to help you do that. Robert's interest in finding the "practical truth" about the area started early, when he was a PhD student himself at Chalmers. At that time there was not much information to be found, or at least the information was not aggregated. So Robert started collecting info and created a knowledge base (that has now grown into [his advice page](https://www.cse.chalmers.se/~feldt/advice/)). He is passionate about giving the "bare truth" to students early so they can find their way in SE and achieve the things, in their careers, that they aspire to.

Robert Feldt is Professor of Software Engineering and Applied AI at Chalmers University of Technology in Gothenburg, where he is part of the Software Engineering division at the Department of Computer Science and Engineering. He is also a part-time Professor at Mid Sweden University. Since April 2017 he has been co-Editor in Chief of one of the major journals in Software Engineering, Springer's [Empirical Software Engineering (EMSE) Journal](https://www.springer.com/journal/10664), and he has been Program Co-Chair of ESEM (2026) and ICST (2018). His current research includes software testing, behavioral software engineering, AI engineering, AI for SE and AI in research itself.

## Course instances
Robert first gave the course at Chalmers University of Technology in Gothenburg during 2009-2012 and then in a short version, focused on journal and conference publication and reviewing, from 2019-2023 at Blekinge Institute of Technology.

During 2023 the course was reworked and extended to be given as a 2-day intensive course at NTNU in Trondheim, Norway. In 2026 it was extended to three days, with new material on research assessment reform, the current strain on peer review, and AI in research, writing and reviewing.
