# Day 2 (Tuesday 3 November 2026): preparation

**What we cover:** how editors and programme committees decide, how to read and review papers, checking whether claims are supported by evidence (including with an AI-based claim–evidence tool), and feedback/clinic on your own work.

**Time:** about 30 minutes of thinking, plus optional reading. Monday evening is the course dinner, so the questions can also be done while travelling on Monday. The reading is optional and short.

## Questions to think about

1. **What should reviewers focus on?** What are the three most important things a reviewer should assess in an SE paper, and why? What should a reviewer put *less* emphasis on?

2. **Ten minutes per paper.** Some big venues now screen papers before full review, with a quick expert judgement on whether a paper has a realistic chance of acceptance. If you had only ten minutes with a paper, which aspects could you judge reliably and which could you not?

3. **Your own paper: claim, evidence, worry.** For the paper you uploaded (or the paper you have read): what is its single most important claim? What evidence supports it? What is your biggest worry about how well the evidence supports the claim? Write this down *before* you see the AI tool's report on Tuesday; we compare your judgement with the tool's. If you haven't selected or uploaded one please contact Robert or we can try to run the tool(s) on your paper during Tuesday (but no promises).

4. **A problem for the paper clinic.** Pick one concrete problem with your paper (or study (if ongoing), or publication plan) that you would like help with on Tuesday afternoon, for example:
   - you cannot state the contribution in one sentence;
   - a claim may be stronger than the evidence;
   - the threats-to-validity section feels generic;
   - you are unsure where to submit;
   - you need to respond to reviews or extend a conference paper into a journal paper.

   Write it as a question of 2–3 sentences and note which part of the paper it concerns. (If you brought someone else's paper: what would you change before resubmitting it, if you were the author?)

## Optional reading (pick one or two, ~30–45 minutes)

- Mary Shaw, ["Writing Good Software Engineering Research Papers"](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~Compose/shaw-icse03.pdf), ICSE 2003. Old, but short and a classic: what kinds of questions, results and validation SE papers present.
- Alan Jay Smith, "The Task of the Referee", IEEE Computer 23(4), 1990 ([published version](https://doi.org/10.1109/2.55470); [free version as Berkeley technical report](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1989/6154.html)). What a referee is actually asked to judge.
- ACM SIGSOFT Empirical Standards, [General Standard](https://github.com/acmsigsoft/EmpiricalStandards/blob/master/docs/standards/GeneralStandard.md). The essential attributes reviewers (might) check in any empirical SE paper. Skim it.
