# Day 3 (Wednesday 4 November 2026): preparation

**What we cover:** research methods and validity, how to make evidence trustworthy when AI can generate plausible text and data, the future of science, and your own AI-use policy and publication strategy.

**Time:** about 30 minutes of thinking on Tuesday evening, plus optional reading. Tuesday's work on your own paper (the claim–evidence report and the clinic) is good input for these questions.

## Questions to think about

1. **Your methods.** Which research methods have you used so far, and which do you plan to use? Why these? Is there an alternative method that could answer your research question differently?

2. **Top-3 validity threats.** For your current or next study: what are the three most serious threats to the validity of your results? What have you done, or could you do, to mitigate them? Did Tuesday's claim–evidence analysis point to any of them?

3. **If your study involves LLMs** (as a tool you evaluate, or as part of your research process, e.g. for coding interview data): what could make your results hard to reproduce or invalid (model versions, non-determinism, prompts, training-data contamination, closed models)?

4. **Could your data be faked?** AI can now generate plausible survey answers, interview transcripts, logs or benchmark results in minutes. If someone fabricated the kind of data *your* study relies on, how could a reader or reviewer tell? What could you provide to make your own evidence more trustworthy (raw data, scripts, provenance, preregistration, replication)?

5. **Your AI use today.** How do you use AI tools in your research, writing and reviewing today? Where do you draw the line, and why? What do you disclose, and to whom?

6. **Publication strategy.** Draft a rough outline: your focus area and goals, the venues you will target (with a reason for each) and how this may change over your PhD or postdoc. We work on it at the end of Wednesday.
