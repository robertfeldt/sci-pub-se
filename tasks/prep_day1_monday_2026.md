# Day 1 (Monday 2 November 2026): preparation

**What we cover:** science and engineering, evidence, how research is assessed, and the publication system under strain.

**No reading is needed for Monday.** (Some optional material is listed at the end, in case you want something for the trip.) Just think about the questions below, for example while travelling, and jot down a few lines for each (on paper or laptop). Roughly 30–45 minutes. There are no right answers; your notes will feed into the discussions in class.

## Questions to think about

1. **Science, engineering and improvement work.** What is science? How does it differ from engineering? And how does a research study differ from an improvement project in a company (for example, finding a better way to catch bugs early in software projects)?

2. **What you believe, and why.** Name one belief that is common in software engineering (in practice or in your research area). What evidence do you actually know of that supports it? Would that evidence convince a sceptic?

3. **Important vs. cited.** Think of two papers in your area that you consider important: one highly cited, one much less cited. Why might their citation counts differ? Does either represent better science? (From memory is fine; no need to look up numbers.)

4. **How are you assessed?** What counts when researchers are hired, promoted or funded where you work: number of papers, venues, citations, external funding, other things? Do you know whether your university or funder has signed DORA or CoARA, and does it make any difference in practice? (Guessing is fine; we compare Norway and Sweden in class.)

5. **Where and why to publish.** What is the difference between publishing at a conference and in a journal? Where have you published, or where do you plan to, and why there?

6. **Rejection.** Have you had a paper desk rejected or rejected after review? If so, what did you learn from it? If not, what would you want to receive from the venue if it happened?
