# San Francisco Declaration on Research Assessment (DORA), 2012 (published May 2013)

**Links:** https://sfdora.org/read/

Drafted at the ASCB annual meeting, San Francisco, Dec 2012. Today ~25k+ signatories (individuals and organisations).

- **One general recommendation** (the core): **do not use journal-based metrics such as the JIF as a surrogate** for the quality of individual articles, individual scientists' contributions, or hiring, promotion and funding decisions.
- **17 further recommendations, addressed to 5 audiences:**
  - **Funders (#2–3) and institutions (#4–5):** be explicit about criteria; *content > metrics or venue*, especially for early-career researchers; value **all outputs, including datasets and software**; use broad, qualitative impact measures.
  - **Publishers (#6–10):** stop promoting the JIF, or show it alongside other metrics; offer article-level metrics; require author-contribution statements (CRediT); open reference lists; no limits on reference counts; cite primary literature.
  - **Metrics providers (#11–14):** transparent data and methods; open licences; no tolerance for gaming; account for article-type and field differences.
  - **Researchers (#15–18):** judge by content when on committees; cite primary sources; use a range of article-level indicators; **challenge JIF-based practices** and teach better ones.
- **Why the JIF fails at article level:** skewed citation distributions (a few papers drive the mean); field-specific; gameable; not reproducible or transparent.
- **Limits of DORA:** a declaration, with no enforcement or monitoring. That gap is what CoARA (2022) addresses with commitments and timelines.

**Teaching hooks**
- Ask Norwegian and Swedish participants whether their university signed (most have) and whether hiring committees still count "level 2" or Q1 papers.
- SE angle: CORE rankings, CSRankings and "A* venue" talk are venue proxies too, the CS equivalent of the JIF.
- #3 (software and datasets as outputs) is especially relevant for SE tool and artifact work.
