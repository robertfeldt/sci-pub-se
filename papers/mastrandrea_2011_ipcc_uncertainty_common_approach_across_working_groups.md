# Mastrandrea, Mach, Plattner, Edenhofer, Stocker, Field, Ebi & Matschoss (2011), "The IPCC AR5 guidance note on consistent treatment of uncertainties: a common approach across the working groups"

**Links:** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10584-011-0178-6 (open access)

Climatic Change 108:675–691 (open access). The *citable, explanatory* companion to the 2010 note.

- **Goal:** one framework and calibrated language across all three IPCC Working Groups (physical science, impacts, mitigation), which have very different kinds of evidence.
- **Main change from AR4:**
  - Replace the earlier parallel scales with a two-step approach: **evidence + agreement → confidence**, then **likelihood** when quantification is possible.
  - Clarify the *relationship* between confidence and likelihood: likelihood is used only with high enough confidence.
- **Recognises** that evidence includes mechanistic understanding, theory, data, models and expert judgement. Not only "data".
- **Discusses challenges:** expert elicitation, conditional findings, the risk-management framing (tails, deep uncertainty), and consistency across chapters through Review Editors.
- **Calibrated language is a communication device.** It doesn't replace expert judgement; it makes the judgement explicit and comparable.

**Teaching hooks**
- Cite this (not the guidance note) on a slide. Use the confidence matrix figure.
- Ask: SE also mixes very different evidence types (proofs, benchmarks, experiments, interviews). Could one common certainty language work across the SE sub-communities?
- Known critique (from memory, not in this paper): readers systematically misinterpret calibrated terms (Budescu et al. 2009/2014). Communication ≠ comprehension.
