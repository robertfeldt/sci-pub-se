# Hicks, Wouters, Waltman, de Rijcke & Rafols (2015), "Bibliometrics: The Leiden Manifesto for research metrics"

**Links:** https://doi.org/10.1038/520429a · http://www.leidenmanifesto.org/

Nature 520:429–431 (23 April 2015). Comment piece by scientometricians: the metric *producers* criticising metric *use*.

- **Problem:** evaluation is increasingly driven by data (JIF, h-index, rankings) rather than judgement. Metrics are applied by non-experts, "with good intentions, not always well informed, often ill applied".
- **10 principles:**
  1. Quantitative evaluation should **support** qualitative expert assessment, not replace it.
  2. Measure performance against the **research mission** of the institution, group or researcher.
  3. Protect excellence in **locally relevant** research (e.g. non-English, regional topics).
  4. Keep data collection and analysis **open, transparent and simple**.
  5. Allow those evaluated to **verify** the data and analysis.
  6. Account for **field variation** in publication and citation practices (normalise; percentiles).
  7. Base assessment of individuals on **qualitative judgement of their portfolio**. The h-index depends on age and field.
  8. Avoid **misplaced concreteness and false precision** (e.g. JIFs to 3 decimals).
  9. Recognise the **systemic effects** of indicators: goals displace, and people game what is measured (Goodhart). Use a suite of indicators.
  10. **Scrutinise and update** indicators regularly.
- Includes the example of **Australia's** publication-count funding (1990s), which increased output but lowered relative impact (Butler 2003), and a figure of the growth of "impact factor" mentions in the literature.

**Teaching hooks**
- Principle 9 plus the Butler example map directly onto **Sweden's 2026 move toward more bibliometric weight**, versus Norway dropping publication points in 2025.
- Principle 3 matters for Norwegian and Swedish SE researchers publishing for local industry.
- Pair with your own bibliometrics slides: which Leiden principle does each metric (h-index, JIF, CORE rank) violate when misused?
