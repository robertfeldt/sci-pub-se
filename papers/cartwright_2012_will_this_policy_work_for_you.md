# Nancy Cartwright (2012), "Presidential Address: Will This Policy Work for You? Predicting Effectiveness Better: How Philosophy Helps"

**Links:** https://doi.org/10.1086/668041 (summary only; PDF not redistributable)

Philosophy of Science 79(5):973–989 (PSA presidential address).

- **Target:** the "takeover movement" in development economics (and evidence-based policy) that demands predictions be based on RCTs.
- **Key distinction:**
  - **efficacy** ("it worked there", in a study population);
  - **effectiveness** ("it will work here", in my setting).
  - RCTs establish the first. Policy needs the second.
- **Running example:** the World Bank's **Bangladesh Integrated Nutrition Project (BINP)**, modelled on the successful **Tamil Nadu (TINP)** project, failed. In Bangladesh, mothers were often not the ones deciding about food: mothers-in-law and men did the shopping. The *support factors* were missing.
- **What's needed beyond the RCT:**
  - (a) evidence that the policy plays the **same causal role** here as there;
  - (b) evidence that the necessary **support factors** are present here.
  - Causes work as *INUS*-style components of causal "cakes": a cause needs its whole team.
- **Role for philosophy of science:** supplying the right *concepts* (causal role, support factors, capacities) for warranting effectiveness predictions. A matter of "vast social importance".

**Teaching hooks**
- SE translation: "TDD/pair programming/code review worked in study X (students, company Y)". What are the support factors in *your* team? This is external validity done properly, not just a threats section.
- Connects to Matsubara & Conte's Evidence-to-Decision frameworks: EtD asks about context too.
