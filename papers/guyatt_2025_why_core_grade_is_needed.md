# Why Core GRADE is needed: introduction to a new series in The BMJ

Guyatt G, Hultcrantz M, Agoritsas T, Iorio A, Vandvik PO, Montori VM. *BMJ* 2025;389:e081902. https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2024-081902

## TLDR
- **GRADE is the dominant system** for rating certainty of evidence and grading the strength of healthcare recommendations: introduced in 2004, required for all Cochrane reviews, endorsed by 120+ organisations (incl. WHO and The BMJ), and extended beyond treatment to diagnosis, prognosis, public/environmental health and qualitative research (GRADE-CERQual).
- **The problem: GRADE has become too complex.** 50+ guidance papers over 20 years, published piecemeal, partly out of date, poorly organised and hard to navigate; new and existing users find it unwieldy.
- **Core GRADE = the essentials in one place.** A seven-paper BMJ series that rationalises, reformulates, extends and modifies GRADE principles, so users no longer need to consult earlier GRADE papers.
- **Scope:** comparing a *single intervention with a single comparator*, from an individual patient's clinical decision-making perspective.
- **Guidance, not rules.** Flexibility is a core principle: more than one best judgment is often defensible, and close-call decisions are common.
- **"Rigorous simplicity" as the inclusion criterion:** guidance that is not necessary for sound judgments about certainty and recommendations is left out, even if it adds elegance, depth or transparency. Left out in particular: guidance with little uptake, that is arcane, changes often or rarely matters in practice.
- **Grounded in experience:** the authors' decades of supervising hundreds of systematic reviews (often by students) and guideline panels with limited GRADE background.
- **Series structure:** (1) overview; (2) target of certainty rating and imprecision; (3) inconsistency; (4) risk of bias, publication bias and rating up; (5) indirectness; (6) summary of findings tables; (7) moving from evidence to recommendations.
- **Not official:** Core GRADE is *not* an official product of the GRADE Working Group, but a collaborative effort by a group of GRADE experts (incl. people behind the MAGIC foundation and BMJ Rapid Recommendations).
