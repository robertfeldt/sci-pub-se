# Ralph (ed.) et al. (2020/21), "ACM SIGSOFT Empirical Standards" (report v0.2.0)

**Links:** https://acmsigsoft.github.io/EmpiricalStandards/ · repo: https://github.com/acmsigsoft/EmpiricalStandards · report: https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.03525

arXiv:2010.03525 · Paper and Peer Review Quality Task Force. Robert Feldt is a contributor (General Standard).

- **Problem:** peer review is unreliable. Criteria like "soundness" or "significance" are vague, so the real criteria stay effectively *secret*. Reviewers disagree and apply ad hoc, personal standards, which leads to arbitrary rejections and long cycles.
- **What a standard is:** a brief (~1-page), public checklist of what the SE community expects from a study of a given method.
- **Three kinds:**
  - the **General Standard**, which applies to every empirical paper;
  - **method-specific** standards (case study, experiment, survey, MSR, grounded theory, systematic review, …);
  - **supplements** (e.g. sampling, open science, information visualisation).
- **Anatomy of a standard:** definition and scope; *essential*, *desirable* and *extraordinary* attributes; general quality criteria; acceptable deviations; antipatterns; **invalid criticisms**; suggested readings; exemplars.
- **Modular:** a mixed-methods paper combines several standards, and review forms are generated from that combination.
- **Two key rules for reviewers:**
  - **Be flexible.** No "gotcha". Ask whether a deviation is justified and whether it can be fixed for camera-ready.
  - **No new criteria.** If an attribute isn't in the standard, don't judge the paper on it.
- **Three adoption levels:**
  - *Accidental:* reviewers start using the standards on their own, which can cause conflict ("why wasn't I told?").
  - *Partial:* the venue references the standards, asks authors to select their methods, trains reviewers, and changes the decision field.
  - *Full / structured review:* method-specific forms, an author pre-submission checklist, and limited free-text fields.
- **Proposed decision logic:** a paper is rejected only if essential attributes are missing AND the deviation is unjustified AND it can't be fixed without new data collection. Otherwise, revise or accept. Reviewers are not asked directly for accept/reject.
- **"Reviews like film critics' essays":** the report argues scientists should evaluate studies against explicit criteria rather than write free-form essays.
- **Caveat (from the report):** the standards *direct* expert judgement, they don't replace it. Words like "reasonable" and "convincing" still need humans. Done badly, they turn into box-ticking.
- **Scientific basis:** analogy to measurement by expert judgement. Explicit rubrics improve inter-rater reliability.
- **Also covers:** costs and benefits, and justice/equity/diversity/inclusion (e.g. fairer treatment of qualitative and non-dominant methods).

**Teaching hooks**
- Pair with Smith (1989): the shift from a referee's tacit virtue to explicit, shared criteria.
- Clinic exercise: authors check their own paper against the General Standard's essential attributes.
- Discussion: does a checklist reduce or increase "Reviewer 2"? Which "invalid criticisms" have participants received?
- Tension with CHI 2027 desk rejects: explicit criteria can be applied mechanically at scale.
