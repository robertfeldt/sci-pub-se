# Papers and readings

Papers used in, or built on for, the course, grouped by seminar theme (see the [slides](../slides/slides_overview.md)). Each entry links to a short summary (TLDR plus teaching hooks); 📄 marks entries where an openly licensed PDF is also stored here. For the rest, the summary links to the publisher or author copy.

All readings are optional. They are here so you can follow up on what we discussed.

## 1. Science, engineering and the strength of evidence (Seminar 1)

*How do other fields judge how much a body of evidence supports a conclusion, and what can SE learn from them?*

- [Dybå & Dingsøyr 2008, Strength of evidence in systematic reviews in SE](dybå_2008_strength_of_evidence_in_sysrevs_in_se.md) 📄: the paper that brought GRADE and evidence strength into SE.
- [Matsubara & Conte 2025, The unfinished business of evidence strength in SE](matsubara_2025_unfinished_business_of_evidence_strength_in_se.md) 📄: almost 20 years later, few SE reviews actually grade their evidence, and those that do use outdated GRADE.
- [Guyatt et al. 2025, Why Core GRADE is needed](guyatt_2025_why_core_grade_is_needed.md) 📄: medicine's dominant evidence-grading system, and why it had to be simplified.
- [Guyatt et al. 2025, Core GRADE 1: overview](guyatt_2025_core_grade_paper1_overview.md) 📄: the two core steps, from certainty of evidence to the strength of a recommendation.
- [Guyatt et al. 2025, Core GRADE 4: rating certainty of evidence](guyatt_2025_core_grade_paper4_rating_certainty_of_evidence.md) 📄: how risk of bias and publication bias lower certainty, and when certainty can be rated up.
- [Lewin et al. 2018, Applying GRADE-CERQual to qualitative evidence synthesis](lewin_2018_applying_GRADE-CERQual_to_qualitative_evidence_synthesis.md) 📄: the GRADE counterpart for qualitative findings, which is what much SE evidence is.
- [Lewin et al. 2018, CERQual paper 2: overall assessment and summary of findings](lewin_2018_GRADE-CERQual_paper2_overall_CERQual_assessment_and_summary_of_findings.md) 📄: how to rate confidence per finding and present it in a Summary of Qualitative Findings table.
- [Wohlin 2013, An evidence profile for SE research and practice](wohlin_2013_evidence_profile_for_se_research_and_practice.md) 📄: an SE-specific way to value heterogeneous evidence, modelled on how courts weigh evidence.
- [Matsubara & Conte 2026, Evidence-to-Decision frameworks for SE](matsubara_2026_evidence_to_decision_frameworks_for_se.md): SE adopted the grading half of GRADE but not the step from evidence to practitioner recommendations.
- [Mastrandrea et al. 2010, IPCC AR5 guidance note on uncertainty](mastrandrea_2010_ipcc_ar5_guidance_note_on_uncertainty.md) 📄: outside medicine, the climate panel's calibrated language for confidence (evidence × agreement) and likelihood.
- [Mastrandrea et al. 2011, IPCC uncertainty: a common approach across working groups](mastrandrea_2011_ipcc_uncertainty_common_approach_across_working_groups.md) 📄: the citable explanation of why the IPCC uses one framework for very different kinds of evidence.
- [IJzerman et al. 2020, Evidence Readiness Levels](ijzerman_2020_evidence_readiness_levels_behavioural_science_to_policy.md) 📄: a TRL-like ladder for when research is ready to use; a good systematic review only reaches level 3.
- [Cartwright & Munro 2010, Limitations of RCTs in predicting effectiveness](cartwright_2010_limitations_of_rcts_in_predicting_effectiveness.md): a trial shows it worked *there*; predicting *here* needs knowledge of causal structure and support factors.
- [Cartwright 2012, Will this policy work for you?](cartwright_2012_will_this_policy_work_for_you.md): efficacy ("it worked there") vs effectiveness ("it will work here"), the gap behind many SE generalisation claims.
- [Cartwright 2013, Why evidence doesn't always travel](cartwright_2013_why_evidence_doesnt_always_travel.md): the two extra facts you need evidence for before a result transfers to a new context.

## 2. Bibliometrics and the reform of research assessment (Seminars 1 and 5)

*How are research and researchers assessed, and how is that changing?*

- [DORA 2012, San Francisco Declaration on Research Assessment](dora_2012_san_francisco_declaration_on_research_assessment.md): don't use journal metrics such as the impact factor to judge individual papers or people.
- [Hicks et al. 2015, The Leiden Manifesto for research metrics](hicks_2015_leiden_manifesto_for_research_metrics.md): ten principles from the metric makers themselves; metrics should support, not replace, expert judgement.
- [CoARA 2022, Agreement on Reforming Research Assessment](coara_2022_agreement_on_reforming_research_assessment.md) 📄: the European coalition that commits signatories to qualitative, peer-review-based assessment, with follow-up and monitoring.
- [UHR 2021, NOR-CAM toolbox for recognition and rewards](uhr_2021_nor-cam_toolbox_for_recognition_and_rewards.md) 📄: Norway's framework for assessing a wider range of academic work; "everybody should not do everything".

## 3. Publication, writing and reviewing (Seminars 2 and 3)

*What makes a good SE paper, and how do reviewers judge it?*

- [Shaw 2003, Writing good SE research papers](shaw_2003_writing_good_se_research_papers.md): the three questions every paper must answer, and the types of results and validation SE reviewers accept.
- [Smith 1990, The Task of the Referee](smith_1990_task_of_the_referee.md): a classic guide to evaluating a paper and writing a referee report; most of it still applies.
- [Ralph et al. 2020, ACM SIGSOFT Empirical Standards](ralph_2020_acm_sigsoft_empirical_standards.md): public, method-specific checklists of what reviewers should (and should not) expect.
- [Munafò et al. 2017, A manifesto for reproducible science](munafo_2017_manifesto_for_reproducible_science.md) 📄: threats across the research cycle and the measures against them; returns in Seminar 5.

## 4. Research methods and validity (Seminar 4)

*How do we choose methods, and how honestly do we report their limits?*

- [Wohlin & Runeson 2021, Selecting research methodology in industry–academia collaboration](wohlin_2021_selecting_research_methods_for_industry_academia_collaboration_in_SE.md) 📄: guidance on choosing between design science, action research and the technology transfer model.
- [Storey et al. 2020, The who, what, how of SE research](storey_2020_who_what_how_of_SE_research_socio_technical_version.md) 📄: SE is socio-technical, yet most papers claiming to help people do not study them.
- [Williams et al. 2019, Methodology Matters](williams_2019_methodology_matters_socio_technical_se.md) 📄: the preprint behind Storey et al. 2020, with an author survey on *why* methods were chosen.
- [Storey et al. 2025, Guiding principles for mixed methods research in SE](storey_2025_guidelines_for_mixed_methods_research_in_SE.md) 📄: if you mix methods, say why, and integrate the results.
- [Lago et al. 2024, Threats to validity: hypocritical or essential?](lago_2024_threats_to_validity_in_SE_hypocritical_or_essential.md) 📄: even ICSE award papers mostly treat threats to validity as a ritual.
- [Robillard et al. 2024, Communicating study design trade-offs in SE](robillard_2024_communicating_study_design_tradeoffs_in_se.md) 📄: replace boilerplate threats with an honest account of the design trade-offs you made.
- [Cassee & Feldt 2025, The garden of forking paths: a multiverse analysis](cassee_2025_garden_of_forking_paths_multiverse_analysis_in_ese.md): defensible analysis choices can flip a published SE result, so justify yours.
- [Frattini et al. 2026, Replications, revisions and reanalyses](frattini_2026_replications_revisions_reanalyses_managing_empirical_evidence.md) 📄: a framework for how new evidence should update old evidence in SE.

## 5. Evidence and trust in our AI future (Seminar 5)

*What happens to evidence and trust when LLMs are used in research, and text is cheap?*

- [Angermeir et al. 2026, Reproducibility of commercial LLM performance in empirical SE](angermeir_2026_reproducibility_of_commercial_llm_performance_in_ese.md): of 85 LLM papers from ICSE/ASE 2024, only 5 could be rerun, and none fully reproduced.
- [Baltes et al. 2026, Guidelines for empirical studies in SE involving LLMs](baltes_2026_guidelines_for_empirical_studies_in_se_involving_llms.md): eight community guidelines on reporting and validating LLM-based studies ([llm-guidelines.org](https://llm-guidelines.org/)).
- Munafò et al. 2017 (above, section 3): registered reports, open data and other defences, revisited for when data can be generated.
- Research-assessment reform (section 2): when papers and reviews become cheap, counts mean even less.
