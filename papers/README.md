# Papers and readings

Papers used in, or built on for, the course, grouped by seminar theme (see the [slides](../slides/slides_overview.md)). Each title links to the paper itself, and the link after each sentence goes to a short summary (TLDR plus teaching hooks). 📄 marks openly licensed PDFs stored in this repo; the other titles link to the publisher, arXiv or author copy.

All readings are optional. They are here so you can follow up on what we discussed.

## 1. Science, engineering and the strength of evidence (Seminar 1)

*How do other fields judge how much a body of evidence supports a conclusion, and what can SE learn from them?*

- [Dybå & Dingsøyr 2008, Strength of evidence in systematic reviews in SE](dybå_2008_strength_of_evidence_in_sysrevs_in_se.pdf) 📄: the paper that brought GRADE and evidence strength into SE. ([summary](dybå_2008_strength_of_evidence_in_sysrevs_in_se.md))
- [Ivarsson & Gorschek 2011, A method for evaluating rigor and industrial relevance](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10664-010-9146-4): a simple SE rubric that scores how well a study is reported (rigor, 0–3) and how close it is to industrial use (relevance, 0–4). ([summary](ivarsson_2011_method_for_evaluating_rigor_and_industrial_relevance.md))
- [Matsubara & Conte 2025, The unfinished business of evidence strength in SE](matsubara_2025_unfinished_business_of_evidence_strength_in_se.pdf) 📄: almost 20 years later, few SE reviews actually grade their evidence, and those that do use outdated GRADE. ([summary](matsubara_2025_unfinished_business_of_evidence_strength_in_se.md))
- [Guyatt et al. 2025, Why Core GRADE is needed](guyatt_2025_why_core_grade_is_needed.pdf) 📄: medicine's dominant evidence-grading system, and why it had to be simplified. ([summary](guyatt_2025_why_core_grade_is_needed.md))
- [Guyatt et al. 2025, Core GRADE 1: overview](guyatt_2025_core_grade_paper1_overview.pdf) 📄: the two core steps, from certainty of evidence to the strength of a recommendation. ([summary](guyatt_2025_core_grade_paper1_overview.md))
- [Guyatt et al. 2025, Core GRADE 4: rating certainty of evidence](guyatt_2025_core_grade_paper4_rating_certainty_of_evidence.pdf) 📄: how risk of bias and publication bias lower certainty, and when certainty can be rated up. ([summary](guyatt_2025_core_grade_paper4_rating_certainty_of_evidence.md))
- [Lewin et al. 2018, Applying GRADE-CERQual to qualitative evidence synthesis](lewin_2018_applying_GRADE-CERQual_to_qualitative_evidence_synthesis.pdf) 📄: the GRADE counterpart for qualitative findings, which is what much SE evidence is. ([summary](lewin_2018_applying_GRADE-CERQual_to_qualitative_evidence_synthesis.md))
- [Lewin et al. 2018, CERQual paper 2: overall assessment and summary of findings](lewin_2018_GRADE-CERQual_paper2_overall_CERQual_assessment_and_summary_of_findings.pdf) 📄: how to rate confidence per finding and present it in a Summary of Qualitative Findings table. ([summary](lewin_2018_GRADE-CERQual_paper2_overall_CERQual_assessment_and_summary_of_findings.md))
- [Wohlin 2013, An evidence profile for SE research and practice](wohlin_2013_evidence_profile_for_se_research_and_practice.pdf) 📄: an SE-specific way to value heterogeneous evidence, modelled on how courts weigh evidence. ([summary](wohlin_2013_evidence_profile_for_se_research_and_practice.md))
- [Matsubara & Conte 2026, Evidence-to-Decision frameworks for SE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.08015): SE adopted the grading half of GRADE but not the step from evidence to practitioner recommendations. ([summary](matsubara_2026_evidence_to_decision_frameworks_for_se.md))
- [Mastrandrea et al. 2010, IPCC AR5 guidance note on uncertainty](mastrandrea_2010_ipcc_ar5_guidance_note_on_uncertainty.pdf) 📄: outside medicine, the climate panel's calibrated language for confidence (evidence × agreement) and likelihood. ([summary](mastrandrea_2010_ipcc_ar5_guidance_note_on_uncertainty.md))
- [Mastrandrea et al. 2011, IPCC uncertainty: a common approach across working groups](mastrandrea_2011_ipcc_uncertainty_common_approach_across_working_groups.pdf) 📄: the citable explanation of why the IPCC uses one framework for very different kinds of evidence. ([summary](mastrandrea_2011_ipcc_uncertainty_common_approach_across_working_groups.md))
- [IJzerman et al. 2020, Evidence Readiness Levels](ijzerman_2020_evidence_readiness_levels_behavioural_science_to_policy.pdf) 📄: a TRL-like ladder for when research is ready to use; a good systematic review only reaches level 3. ([summary](ijzerman_2020_evidence_readiness_levels_behavioural_science_to_policy.md))
- [Cartwright & Munro 2010, Limitations of RCTs in predicting effectiveness](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2753.2010.01382.x): a trial shows it worked *there*; predicting *here* needs knowledge of causal structure and support factors. ([summary](cartwright_2010_limitations_of_rcts_in_predicting_effectiveness.md))
- [Cartwright 2012, Will this policy work for you?](https://doi.org/10.1086/668041): efficacy ("it worked there") vs effectiveness ("it will work here"), the gap behind many SE generalisation claims. ([summary](cartwright_2012_will_this_policy_work_for_you.md))
- [Cartwright 2013, Why evidence doesn't always travel](https://doi.org/10.1332/174426413X662581): the two extra facts you need evidence for before a result transfers to a new context. ([summary](cartwright_2013_why_evidence_doesnt_always_travel.md))

## 2. Bibliometrics and the reform of research assessment (Seminars 1 and 5)

*How are research and researchers assessed, and how is that changing?*

- [DORA 2012, San Francisco Declaration on Research Assessment](https://sfdora.org/read/): don't use journal metrics such as the impact factor to judge individual papers or people. ([summary](dora_2012_san_francisco_declaration_on_research_assessment.md))
- [Hicks et al. 2015, The Leiden Manifesto for research metrics](https://www.nature.com/articles/520429a.pdf): ten principles from the metric makers themselves; metrics should support, not replace, expert judgement. ([summary](hicks_2015_leiden_manifesto_for_research_metrics.md))
- [CoARA 2022, Agreement on Reforming Research Assessment](coara_2022_agreement_on_reforming_research_assessment.pdf) 📄: the European coalition that commits signatories to qualitative, peer-review-based assessment, with follow-up and monitoring. ([summary](coara_2022_agreement_on_reforming_research_assessment.md))
- [UHR 2021, NOR-CAM toolbox for recognition and rewards](uhr_2021_nor-cam_toolbox_for_recognition_and_rewards.pdf) 📄: Norway's framework for assessing a wider range of academic work; "everybody should not do everything". ([summary](uhr_2021_nor-cam_toolbox_for_recognition_and_rewards.md))

## 3. Publication, writing and reviewing (Seminars 2 and 3)

*What makes a good SE paper, and how do reviewers judge it?*

- [Shaw 2003, Writing good SE research papers](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~Compose/shaw-icse03.pdf): the three questions every paper must answer, and the types of results and validation SE reviewers accept. ([summary](shaw_2003_writing_good_se_research_papers.md))
- [Smith 1990, The Task of the Referee](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1989/6154.html): a classic guide to evaluating a paper and writing a referee report; most of it still applies. ([summary](smith_1990_task_of_the_referee.md))
- [Ralph et al. 2020, ACM SIGSOFT Empirical Standards](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.03525): public, method-specific checklists of what reviewers should (and should not) expect. ([summary](ralph_2020_acm_sigsoft_empirical_standards.md))
- [Munafò et al. 2017, A manifesto for reproducible science](munafo_2017_manifesto_for_reproducible_science.pdf) 📄: threats across the research cycle and the measures against them; returns in Seminar 5. ([summary](munafo_2017_manifesto_for_reproducible_science.md))

## 4. Research methods and validity (Seminar 4)

*How do we choose methods, and how honestly do we report their limits?*

- [Wohlin & Runeson 2021, Selecting research methodology in industry–academia collaboration](wohlin_2021_selecting_research_methods_for_industry_academia_collaboration_in_SE.pdf) 📄: guidance on choosing between design science, action research and the technology transfer model. ([summary](wohlin_2021_selecting_research_methods_for_industry_academia_collaboration_in_SE.md))
- [Storey et al. 2020, The who, what, how of SE research](storey_2020_who_what_how_of_SE_research_socio_technical_version.pdf) 📄: SE is socio-technical, yet most papers claiming to help people do not study them. ([summary](storey_2020_who_what_how_of_SE_research_socio_technical_version.md))
- [Williams et al. 2019, Methodology Matters](williams_2019_methodology_matters_socio_technical_se.pdf) 📄: the preprint behind Storey et al. 2020, with an author survey on *why* methods were chosen. ([summary](williams_2019_methodology_matters_socio_technical_se.md))
- [Storey et al. 2025, Guiding principles for mixed methods research in SE](storey_2025_guidelines_for_mixed_methods_research_in_SE.pdf) 📄: if you mix methods, say why, and integrate the results. ([summary](storey_2025_guidelines_for_mixed_methods_research_in_SE.md))
- [Lago et al. 2024, Threats to validity: hypocritical or essential?](lago_2024_threats_to_validity_in_SE_hypocritical_or_essential.pdf) 📄: even ICSE award papers mostly treat threats to validity as a ritual. ([summary](lago_2024_threats_to_validity_in_SE_hypocritical_or_essential.md))
- [Robillard et al. 2024, Communicating study design trade-offs in SE](robillard_2024_communicating_study_design_tradeoffs_in_se.pdf) 📄: replace boilerplate threats with an honest account of the design trade-offs you made. ([summary](robillard_2024_communicating_study_design_tradeoffs_in_se.md))
- [Cassee & Feldt 2025, The garden of forking paths: a multiverse analysis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.08910): defensible analysis choices can flip a published SE result, so justify yours. ([summary](cassee_2025_garden_of_forking_paths_multiverse_analysis_in_ese.md))
- [Frattini et al. 2026, Replications, revisions and reanalyses](frattini_2026_replications_revisions_reanalyses_managing_empirical_evidence.pdf) 📄: a framework for how new evidence should update old evidence in SE. ([summary](frattini_2026_replications_revisions_reanalyses_managing_empirical_evidence.md))

## 5. Evidence and trust in our AI future (Seminar 5)

*What happens to evidence and trust when LLMs are used in research, and text is cheap?*

- [Angermeir et al. 2026, Reproducibility of commercial LLM performance in empirical SE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.25506): of 85 LLM papers from ICSE/ASE 2024, only 5 could be rerun, and none fully reproduced. ([summary](angermeir_2026_reproducibility_of_commercial_llm_performance_in_ese.md))
- [Baltes et al. 2026, Guidelines for empirical studies in SE involving LLMs](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.15503): eight community guidelines on reporting and validating LLM-based studies ([llm-guidelines.org](https://llm-guidelines.org/)). ([summary](baltes_2026_guidelines_for_empirical_studies_in_se_involving_llms.md))
- Munafò et al. 2017 (above, section 3): registered reports, open data and other defences, revisited for when data can be generated.
- Research-assessment reform (section 2): when papers and reviews become cheap, counts mean even less.
