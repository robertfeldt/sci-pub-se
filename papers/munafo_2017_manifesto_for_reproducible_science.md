# Munafò, Nosek, Bishop, Button, Chambers, Percie du Sert, Simonsohn, Wagenmakers, Ware, Ioannidis (2017), "A manifesto for reproducible science"

**Links:** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-016-0021 (open access)

*Nature Human Behaviour* 1:0021, open access.

- **Problem:** much published research is likely false. Causes: low statistical power, small effects, p-hacking and data dredging, HARKing (hypothesising after the results are known), conflicts of interest, competitive silos. Estimates cited: 85% of biomedical research effort wasted; 90% of *Nature* survey respondents saw a "reproducibility crisis".
- **Root cause is human cognition:** apophenia (seeing patterns in noise), confirmation bias and hindsight bias, amplified by fast, flexible, automated analysis. Examples: almost as many unique fMRI pipelines as studies; ~7,000 pipelines on one fMRI dataset made >90% of voxels "significant" in at least one analysis. "Not scientific discovery, but self-deception."
- **Figure 1, threats across the research cycle:** hypothesis → design → data collection → analysis → interpretation → publication: failure to control for bias, low power, poor quality control, p-hacking, HARKing, publication bias, lack of replication.
- **Measures (Table 1), by category, with estimated adoption in 2017:**
  - **Methods:** protect against cognitive biases (blinding, pre-registration); methods and statistics training; independent methodological support; team science and multi-site studies.
  - **Reporting & dissemination:** pre-registration and **Registered Reports**; reporting checklists; conflict-of-interest disclosure.
  - **Reproducibility:** open data, materials and software; pre-registration (standard in clinical trials, rare elsewhere).
  - **Evaluation:** diversify peer review (preprints, post-publication review).
  - **Incentives:** open science badges (e.g. the badge introduced by *Psychological Science* sharply raised data sharing); Registered Reports; TOP guidelines; funding replications; open practices in hiring and promotion. Links to Monday's research-assessment discussion.
- **Key distinction:** confirmatory (data-independent, hypothesis-testing) vs exploratory (data-contingent, hypothesis-generating) research. Both are valuable; the problem is presenting exploration as confirmation.
- **Caveat by the authors:** the measures themselves need evaluation (metascience); replication alone may not remove false results.
- **Closing quote:** Feynman: "The first principle is that you must not fool yourself – and you are the easiest person to fool."
