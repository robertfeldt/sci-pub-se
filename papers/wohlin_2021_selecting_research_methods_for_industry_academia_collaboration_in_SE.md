# Guiding the selection of research methodology in industry–academia collaboration in software engineering

Wohlin C, Runeson P. *Information and Software Technology* 140:106678, 2021. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.infsof.2021.106678

## TLDR
- **Problem:** SE has a growing literature on research methods, but little guidance on *which methodology to choose* for a given study, especially in collaboration with industry.
- **Terminology:** a *paradigm* (shared beliefs, Kuhn) frames a *methodology* (the overall research approach and its justification), which uses *methods* (how data are collected and analysed). The boundaries are fuzzy and vary between disciplines.
- **Three methodologies compared:**
  - **Design Science:** knowledge through building and evaluating artefacts; contributions as field-tested, grounded "technological rules" (van Aken).
  - **Action Research:** the researcher takes part in a change in the organisation, in iterative cycles (incl. Staron's SE guidebook).
  - **Technology Transfer Model:** from SE (Gorschek et al.): problem identification → academic validation → static validation (with practitioners) → dynamic validation (pilots) → release. Illustrated with Briand et al.'s adaptation.
- **Common frame:** all three map onto the research cycle **describe → solve → practice**, but they are driven by different main objectives.
- **Guidance (by primary objective):**
  - knowledge from developing *tangible artefacts* → start with **design science**;
  - supporting *change in ways of working*, with the researcher part of the team making the change → **action research**;
  - *transferring a research result* to industry without being part of the team → **technology transfer model**.
  - Choose one as the main starting point, but let the strengths of the others influence the research.
- **Conclusion:** the important thing is to make an *informed, explicit* choice of methodology. Many studies borrow loosely from these methodologies without following them fully.

## Teaching hooks
- Concrete version of S4 slide 4 ("method selection depends on…"): purpose first, then methodology, then methods.
- Speaks directly to participants doing industry collaboration or coming from IS, where design science and action research are standard.
- Pairs with the ABC framework (strategies) and Petersen & Gencel's worldviews (paradigms): paradigm → methodology → method.
- Question: which of the three is your study closest to? Are you *in* the team making the change, or outside it?
