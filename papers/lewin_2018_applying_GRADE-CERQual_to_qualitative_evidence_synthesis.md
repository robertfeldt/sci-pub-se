# Applying GRADE-CERQual to qualitative evidence synthesis findings: introduction to the series

Lewin S, Booth A, Glenton C, et al. (13 authors). *Implementation Science* 2018;13(Suppl 1):2. https://doi.org/10.1186/s13012-017-0688-3 (open access)

## TLDR
- **What CERQual is:** "Confidence in the Evidence from Reviews of Qualitative research", an approach for judging how much confidence to place in *findings* from systematic reviews of qualitative research (qualitative evidence syntheses). Developed since 2010 by a project group within, and in agreement with, the GRADE Working Group. Complements GRADE, which covers evidence on effectiveness, harms, resources and diagnostic tests.
- **Why it's needed:** decisions on interventions and policies need more than effect estimates: values, acceptability, feasibility, implementation. Qualitative syntheses supply this and are used by WHO, NICE and others, but before CERQual there was no accepted, structured way to assess confidence in their findings. Informal, intuitive judgments are neither transparent nor consistent across assessors.
- **Unit of assessment = each individual review finding**, defined as an analytic output (e.g. a theme or theory) that, based on primary-study data, describes a phenomenon or an aspect of one.
- **Confidence = the extent to which a review finding is a reasonable representation of the phenomenon of interest**, i.e. how likely it is to be different enough from reality to change a decision. Four levels: high (highly likely a reasonable representation), moderate (likely), low (possible), very low (not clear).
- **Four components:**
  1. *Methodological limitations:* concerns about the design or conduct of the contributing primary studies.
  2. *Coherence:* how clear and cogent the fit is between the primary data and the review finding.
  3. *Adequacy of data:* richness and quantity of the supporting data.
  4. *Relevance:* how well the supporting evidence applies to the context in the review question (perspective/population, phenomenon, setting).

  A possible fifth, *dissemination (publication) bias*, is under exploration (paper 7).
- **Output:** a *Summary of Qualitative Findings* table (each finding, its confidence, why) backed by a *CERQual Evidence Profile* (explicit judgments per component). Minimum criteria exist for claiming fidelity to CERQual.
- **Stated assumptions:** a "subtle realist" stance (social reality is not entirely constructed, so synthesis across studies can be trustworthy and deeper than single studies); qualitative research has an instrumental role in informing decisions; CERQual should only be applied to well-conducted, transparently reported syntheses; users need good knowledge of qualitative methods and synthesis.
- **Acknowledged limitation:** confidence is a continuum, so cutting it into four levels is somewhat arbitrary; the authors judge accessibility and transparency to outweigh this.
- **Method-agnostic by design:** independent of specific primary qualitative methods and synthesis methods. Experience so far is mostly with aggregative, descriptive findings in health and social care.
- **Open research agenda:** mixed-methods syntheses; presenting CERQual alongside GRADE assessments; sources not produced by formal qualitative research (blogs, online discussions, news); fields outside health; more interpretive outputs (logic models, meta-ethnography). The authors explicitly invite use in other fields.
- **Series map:** paper 2 = overall assessment and summary tables; papers 3–6 = one component each (methodological limitations, coherence, adequacy, relevance); paper 7 = dissemination bias.
